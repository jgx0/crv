\title{Challenge Run Value ($cRV$): quantifying strategic value in MLB's ABS
challenge era}
\runningtitle{Challenge Run Value in MLB's ABS challenge era}

% Author block. Filled from title_page.md.
\author*[1]{Jacob Goldstein}
\runningauthor{J.~Goldstein}
\affil[1]{\protect\raggedright
Millburn High School, Millburn, NJ 07041, United States, e-mail: 27goldsteinj@millburn.org, ORCID: 0009-0004-2659-1256}

\abstract{We introduce Challenge Run Value ($cRV$), a pitch-level metric for
valuing Automated Ball-Strike (ABS) challenges in Major League Baseball. The
model combines run-expectancy deltas from count- and plate-appearance-ending
state changes with a time-decaying opportunity-cost penalty for failed
challenges. Unlike framing metrics, which measure physical receiving skill
under human umpiring, $cRV$ treats the challenge as a scarce strategic
resource whose value depends on timing, conversion, and forgone future
options. We implement a reproducible five-module pipeline---ingestion, event
isolation, alternate-state generation, RE288 mapping, and scoring---with
bootstrap uncertainty, leverage stratification, and sensitivity analysis. A
central methodological contribution is the valuation of terminal outcomes:
challenges converting a called strike three into ball four, or the reverse,
end the plate appearance and must be valued from post-event base/out states
and runs scored, not zeroed placeholders. Applying the pipeline to the
$1{,}971$ plate-appearance-ending challenges recoverable from the 2026 regular
season through August 12, we estimate a mean $cRV$ of $0.145$ runs per
challenge (game-clustered bootstrap $95\%$ CI $[0.132,\,0.157]$), with
catchers generating more than three times the per-challenge value of batters
($0.223$ versus $0.067$ runs) in this terminal-challenge sample. The highest-value events are terminal
conversions---bases-loaded walk overturns and inning-ending strikeout
overturns worth over $1.7$ runs each---and zeroing such states, as a naive
implementation would, drives the aggregate negative ($-0.031$), inverting the
sign of the result.}

\keywords{run expectancy; opportunity cost; catcher framing; sabermetrics;

\section*{Data availability}
The pitch-level inputs are publicly available Statcast data, retrieved through
the \texttt{pybaseball} interface \citep{pybaseball}. The results reported in
this manuscript are computed from the 2026 regular-season challenge corpus
through August 12 ($n = 1{,}971$) extracted from those feeds. The deterministic synthetic
validation corpus described in Section~\ref{sec:validation} is retained as an
offline correctness harness and is regenerable from the accompanying code.
