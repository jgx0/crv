\title{Challenge Run Value ($cRV$): quantifying strategic value in MLB's ABS
challenge era}
\runningtitle{Challenge Run Value in MLB's ABS challenge era}

\abstract{We introduce Challenge Run Value ($cRV$), a pitch-level metric for
valuing Automated Ball-Strike (ABS) challenges in Major League Baseball.
Unlike framing metrics, which measure receiving skill under human umpiring,
$cRV$ treats the challenge as a scarce strategic resource: it combines
run-expectancy deltas from count- and plate-appearance-ending state changes
with a time-decaying, scarcity-weighted penalty for failed challenges. A
central contribution is the valuation of terminal outcomes, such as
challenges converting a called strike three into ball four (or the reverse),
which must be priced from post-event base/out states and runs scored rather
than zeroed. Applying the model to the $1{,}971$ plate-appearance-ending
challenges recoverable from the 2026 regular season through August 12, we
estimate a mean $cRV$ of $0.159$ runs per challenge (bootstrap $95\%$ CI
$[0.147,\,0.172]$); catchers generate roughly two-and-a-half times the
per-challenge value of batters ($0.231$ versus $0.088$). Zeroing terminal
states would instead drive the aggregate negative ($-0.016$), inverting the
sign of the result.}

\keywords{run expectancy; opportunity cost; catcher framing; sabermetrics;
decision quality; resource allocation}

\section*{Data availability}
The pitch-level inputs are publicly available Statcast data, retrieved through
the \texttt{pybaseball} interface \citep{pybaseball}. The results reported in
this manuscript are computed from the 2026 regular-season challenge corpus
through August 12 ($n = 1{,}971$) extracted from those feeds. The deterministic synthetic
validation corpus described in Section~\ref{sec:validation} is retained as an
offline correctness harness and is regenerable from the accompanying code.
