\abstract{We introduce Challenge Run Value ($cRV$), a pitch-level metric for
valuing Automated Ball-Strike (ABS) challenges in Major League Baseball. The
model combines run-expectancy deltas from count- and plate-appearance-ending
state changes with a time-decaying opportunity-cost penalty for failed
challenges. Unlike framing metrics, which measure physical receiving skill
under human umpiring, $cRV$ treats the challenge as a scarce strategic
resource whose value depends on timing, conversion, and forgone future
options.

We implement a reproducible five-module pipeline---ingestion, event isolation,
alternate-state generation, RE288 mapping, and scoring---with bootstrap
uncertainty, leverage stratification, and sensitivity analysis. A central
methodological contribution is the valuation of terminal outcomes: challenges
converting a called strike three into ball four, or the reverse, end the plate
appearance and must be valued from post-event base/out states and runs scored,
not zeroed placeholders.

Applying the pipeline to $1{,}914$ challenge events from the 2026 regular
season, we estimate a mean $cRV$ of $0.143$ runs per challenge (bootstrap
$95\%$ CI $[0.130,\,0.158]$), with catchers generating more than three times
the per-challenge value of batters ($0.222$ versus $0.065$ runs). The
highest-value events are terminal conversions---bases-loaded walk overturns and
inning-ending strikeout overturns worth over $1.7$ runs each---and zeroing such
states, as a naive implementation would, drives the aggregate negative
($-0.031$), inverting the sign of the result.}

\section*{Data Availability}
The pitch-level inputs are publicly available Statcast data, retrieved through
the \texttt{pybaseball} interface \citep{pybaseball}. The results reported in
this manuscript are computed from the full 2026 regular-season challenge corpus
($n = 1{,}914$) extracted from those feeds. The deterministic synthetic
validation corpus described in Section~\ref{sec:validation} is retained as an
offline correctness harness and is regenerable from the accompanying code.
