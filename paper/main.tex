\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We introduce Challenge Run Value ($cRV$), a pitch-level metric for valuing
Automated Ball-Strike (ABS) challenges in Major League Baseball. The model
combines run-expectancy deltas from count- and plate-appearance-ending state
changes with a time-decaying opportunity-cost penalty for failed challenges.
Unlike framing metrics, which measure physical receiving skill under human
umpiring, $cRV$ treats the challenge as a scarce strategic resource whose
value depends on timing, conversion, and forgone future options.

We implement a reproducible five-module pipeline---ingestion, event isolation,
alternate-state generation, RE288 mapping, and scoring---with bootstrap
uncertainty, leverage stratification, and sensitivity analysis. A central
methodological contribution is the valuation of terminal outcomes: challenges
converting a called strike three into ball four, or the reverse, end the plate
appearance and must be valued from post-event base/out states and runs scored,
not zeroed placeholders.

Applying the pipeline to $1{,}914$ challenge events from the 2026 regular
season, we estimate a mean $cRV$ of $0.143$ runs per challenge (bootstrap
$95\%$ CI $[0.130,\,0.158]$), with catchers generating more than three times
the per-challenge value of batters ($0.222$ versus $0.065$ runs). The
highest-value events are terminal conversions---bases-loaded walk overturns and
inning-ending strikeout overturns worth over $1.7$ runs each---and zeroing such
states, as a naive implementation would, drives the aggregate negative
($-0.031$), inverting the sign of the result.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Data Availability}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Data Availability}
The pitch-level inputs are publicly available Statcast data, retrieved through
the \texttt{pybaseball} interface \citep{pybaseball}. The results reported in
this manuscript are computed from the full 2026 regular-season challenge corpus
($n = 1{,}914$) extracted from those feeds. The deterministic synthetic
validation corpus described in Section~\ref{sec:validation} is retained as an
offline correctness harness and is regenerable from the accompanying code. All
analysis code, including the figure and table generation, will be made publicly
available on acceptance.

\section*{Figure Legends}

\noindent\textbf{Figure 1.} Five-module $cRV$ pipeline: ingestion, event
isolation, alternate-state generation, RE288 mapping, and scoring. Each stage
writes an inspectable intermediate file, so any challenge event can be traced
from its raw description text through to its final score.

\vspace{1em}
\noindent\textbf{Figure 2.} Player-level cumulative $cRV$ for the top
challengers of the 2026 regular season. Bars extending right of the zero rule
(blue) are positive and arise entirely from successful conversions; bars
extending left (red) are negative and arise entirely from opportunity-cost
penalties on failed challenges. Bar direction relative to the zero rule encodes
the sign independently of color, so the comparison survives grayscale
reproduction. The leaderboard is dominated by catchers, the primary users of
the challenge.

\vspace{1em}
\noindent\textbf{Figure 3.} Cumulative $cRV$ aggregated by challenger role
(batter, catcher, and a small number of events whose role could not be parsed).
Batters and catchers issue challenges in nearly equal numbers, but catchers
account for the large majority of aggregate value, converting borderline
two-strike calls into inning-ending outs.

\vspace{1em}
\noindent\textbf{Figure 4.} Total corpus $cRV$ as a function of the baseline
retained-challenge value $EV_c$, over the range $[0.05, 0.30]$ runs. The
relationship is exactly linear because the opportunity-cost penalty applies
only to failed challenges and scales linearly in $EV_c$; successful challenges
carry no penalty term. Endpoint values are labeled.
